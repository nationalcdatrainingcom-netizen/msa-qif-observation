This record draws on two bodies of evidence. They differ in kind, and the paper may want to treat them differently.

# Part One: Concept to Redesign (ChatGPT record, 2025 to early 2026)

## 1. The earliest concept: teacher support and retention

The earliest recoverable MSA work dates to **April 2025**, when Mary Wardlaw and Rebecca Munlyn began building what became Mentor Success Academy.

The problem was larger than training new employees. Years at The Children's Center and National CDA Training had shown Mary that teachers could finish coursework and hold technical knowledge, yet still struggle once they were running a real classroom. New and developing teachers needed someone beside them to help turn knowledge into practice.

From the start, the thinking connected to teacher **confidence, competence, support, development and retention**. Mentoring was how that support would be delivered.

The early concept:

> Train strong, experienced early childhood teachers to become mentors, pair them with new or developing teachers, and give each pair a structured 12-week process instead of leaving mentoring to happen informally.

Mentees were not only brand-new teachers. They could also be new hires, teachers who were struggling, and promising employees being developed as future leaders.

## 2. The original 12-week curriculum was early childhood content

The first MSA curriculum looked very different from the later program. Its 12 weeks were built around early childhood professional competencies and CDA-related areas. The mentoring relationship was mainly the way that content reached teachers.

Early topic areas:

1. Safe and nurturing learning environments
2. Whole child growth and physical wellbeing
3. Emotional and social development
4. Family relationships
5. Intentional classroom organization
6. Reflective and ethical teaching
7. Observation and documentation
8. Child development and learning
9. Play and exploration
10. Routines and transitions
11. Meaningful learning experiences
12. Communication

By **April 7, 2025**, these had become weekly themes:

| Week | Theme |
|---|---|
| 1 | Creating Safe & Supportive Learning Spaces |
| 2 | Nurturing Healthy Bodies & Active Learners |
| 3 | Encouraging Expression, Discovery & Communication |
| 4 | Building Strong Social Roots & Emotional Resilience |
| 5 | Honoring Identity & Partnering with Families |
| 6 | Strengthening Family Connections & Community Ties |
| 7 | Running Smooth Classrooms & Collaborating with Care |
| 8 | Professional Responsibility & Advocacy in Action |
| 9 | Growing Your Professional Self |
| 10 | Seeing Children Clearly: Observation to Action |
| 11 | Supporting Individual Needs with Care & Intention |
| 12 | Understanding Development Through Theory & Practice |

This curriculum answered the question **"What does an early childhood teacher need to know and do?"** Later work increasingly answered a different one: **"What does an effective mentor need to know and do to help another teacher grow?"** That change in the guiding question is one of the most important shifts in MSA's history.

## 3. The original operating model: two experts, two contributions

By **April 8, 2025**, Mary and Rebecca had divided the work.

- **Mary:** early childhood pedagogy and technical content, plus most of the infrastructure (Kajabi, the website, costs and implementation support).
- **Rebecca:** the mentor and mentee relationship, emotional support, pairing, coaching, and weekly mentor development and Q&A.

That division points to what eventually happened. At first, the teaching content and the mentoring skills sat side by side. Over time, the mentoring and relationship work moved to the center of the product.

The Children's Center would be the real-world pilot, followed by a lower-cost external beta. Early business discussions included live and recorded sessions, ongoing check-ins, a 50/50 profit arrangement by mutual agreement, and possibly a co-authored book.

## 4. A highly structured delivery system

By **mid-April 2025**, the delivery model was taking shape. MSA would run in 12-week cycles, possibly four a year. The weekly commitment was kept deliberately small:

- **30 minutes of mentor training**, plus
- **30 minutes of paid mentor and mentee time**

That model recognized something that stayed true through every later version: mentoring cannot simply be added to a teacher's workload with no structure and no protected time.

The stated goals:

- teacher confidence and competence
- strong mentor relationships
- greater employee retention
- whole-teacher support, including wellbeing and communication
- an internal pipeline of teachers who could become mentors and leaders themselves

## 5. The first public version of MSA

By **late April and early May 2025**, MSA was a recognizable product, not just an internal idea. The offer included:

- 12 weeks of guided mentoring with trained mentors
- structured coaching and goal setting
- downloadable tools and templates
- conversation guides and practice-based strategies
- reflection and accountability logs
- personalized feedback
- mentor check-ins and coaching
- a mentor community
- continued access to materials and updates
- a completion certificate

The language now stressed relationship-based mentoring and long-term teacher confidence and success. MSA was no longer only a curriculum. It was becoming a system built around the mentoring relationship.

## 6. The first real-world implementation

By **May 31, 2025**, MSA was moving into practice across Mary's centers. The plan called for about three mentors per center, each paired with a teacher for the 12-week process. After that, mentors would keep getting monthly training and support, and new mentees could join later cohorts.

This introduced a self-sustaining internal cycle:

> Develop mentors → mentors develop teachers → developing teachers become stronger practitioners → some become mentors → the organization builds its own capacity instead of constantly starting over.

The long-term aim included lead teachers, CDA candidates and associates with degrees, as well as new and developing staff, until they too were mentor-ready. Rebecca's role included weekly paid mentor training, guided conversations, reflection, and mentor support and coaching as needed.

## 7. The 5 Voices framework changed the direction of the curriculum

A major change came by **June 2025**. Instead of opening with technical ECE content, the curriculum now opened with **self-awareness, communication and understanding people**, through the 5 Voices framework.

- **Week 1** became "Mentoring Through the Lens of the 5 Voices." Mentors identified their natural leadership voice and looked at how it shaped the way they communicated, led, read situations and worked with their mentee. There were separate reflection tools for the mentor and for the mentor and mentee's classroom reflection together.
- **Week 2** looked more closely at how the mentor's natural voice affected leadership, mentoring, communication and support for the mentee.
- **Week 3** became "Know Yourself to Lead Yourself & Others": self-awareness, natural tendencies, intentional behavior, reflection, sharing, and learning with a partner.

This was a major departure from the April curriculum. The program was starting to recognize that **a technically excellent teacher does not automatically become an excellent mentor.** A mentor has to understand herself and the other person, adjust how she communicates, build trust, listen, reflect, give feedback and help someone find their own solutions. That realization steadily changed what MSA was training.

## 8. Classroom content stayed, but its role changed

ECE content remained important. Week 1's classroom reflection, for example, looked at the learning environment: space, materials, routines, relationships, trust, play, exploration and social interaction.

But these classroom elements were no longer mainly things to teach. They became material for observation, discussion and reflective mentoring.

- **Earlier model:** teach the teacher the correct practice.
- **Developing model:** train the mentor to observe practice, draw the teacher into reflection, and help the teacher improve.

(Section 18 shows how fully the platform later carried out this shift.)

## 9. From weekly class to blended learning system

By **later 2025**, the delivery model had grown. The Training Library / Mentor Series held recorded training, slides, reflection sheets, practice prompts, and materials mentors could return to on their own. Someone who missed a session could watch it later and complete the reflection instead of losing that week.

The model now combined:

> live instruction + recorded learning + individual reflection + mentor/mentee conversation + classroom application + practice + follow-up

MSA was moving from one-time training events toward an ongoing professional learning system.

## 10. Connection to the larger workforce development system

In **late 2025**, while building The Children's Center's broader employee development system, MSA became tied to:

- orientation and onboarding
- CDA preparation
- apprenticeship
- professional development
- classroom competency
- leadership development
- ongoing professional learning communities

One proposed design was a **10-day orientation followed by the full 12-week mentoring experience**, with 5 Voices activities, classroom application, director checkpoints, and CDA and apprenticeship alignment. Four related documents were discussed: a Staff Handbook, an Orientation Guide, a Mentorship Guide and an Apprenticeship Pathway Guide.

At one point the 12 weeks were framed as a developmental sequence:

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Orientation and safety |
| 3–6 | Classroom skills and curriculum |
| 7–10 | Professional growth and mentoring |
| 11–12 | Competency and independence |

Learning packets could include learning material, activities and reflection, observable practice, and mentor or director sign-off. In this period MSA was working as part of a larger teacher development ecosystem, not as a standalone product.

## 11. Reflection became more deliberate

Reflection was there from the start, but its purpose became more refined. At first, reflection sheets mostly reinforced each week's training. Later, two very different kinds of reflection were separated:

- **Developmental reflection:** helps the mentor or mentee think, learn, communicate and grow.
- **Program evaluation:** tells MSA whether the training itself is working.

This distinction mattered especially in the 2026 redesign. Mary did not want participants to feel that every reflective answer they gave their mentor was also being collected as a product rating. The relational safety of reflection had to be protected.

(Section 20 shows how the platform built this separation into its design.)

## 12. The 2026 redesign: the mentor becomes the primary learner

In the major redesign of **April 2026**, one of the clearest decisions was that **the mentor is the main person the training is for.**

The original system could be read as MSA training teachers *through* mentors. The newer system trains the mentor to mentor well, and the mentee grows through that relationship.

So the mentor needs more than ECE expertise. The mentor needs skill in relationship building, self-awareness, communication, observation, reflection, questioning, feedback, coaching, accountability, emotional intelligence, collaboration, leadership, and turning learning into classroom practice. The 5 Voices framework stayed an important foundation for understanding communication style, self-awareness and differences between people.

## 13. A deliberate decision not to build all 12 weeks at once

A key methods decision in **April 2026** was to stop trying to perfect all 12 weeks together. The decision, in short: **perfect Week 1 first.**

Week 1 would become the model of what an excellent MSA week contains. Once its instructional design, mentor experience, mentee interaction, reflection, accountability and data collection all worked, that design could be repeated through the remaining weeks. At that point Module 1 and the high-level Week 1 to 12 framework were still being refined.

MSA was applying its own principle to itself: **reflection before repetition.**

(Section 19 shows the result: a Week 1 template copied across all 12 weeks.)

## 14. Technology and data join the system

The 2026 work added a deliberate data component: electronic reflections with 1-to-5 ratings, stored in one place so patterns could be analyzed later. But quantitative program feedback and relational reflection were not to be merged into a single instrument.

The system therefore planned for:

- electronic reflection with centrally stored responses
- 1-to-5 measures where appropriate
- open-ended responses
- separate program evaluation
- tracking participant experience over time

At first, Mary preferred people, not AI, to review the data and reflections. AI might later help find themes and issues at scale, but the first rollout would let her understand what responses actually meant before automating any interpretation. **Technology would support mentoring, not replace the human relationship that makes it work.**

## 15. The open design questions became system questions

By the time the intensive ChatGPT design work paused, the open questions were no longer "What should we teach in Week 4?" They were system-level:

- How much accountability should MSA require?
- What should the mentor submit, and what should stay private between mentor and mentee?
- How much should be automated, and where is human coaching essential?
- What data shows the program is working?
- How do we keep reflection psychologically and relationally safe while still measuring implementation?
- How much technical ECE content belongs in MSA itself, and how much should be applied through mentoring?
- How standardized should the mentor protocol be?
- What is core MSA content, and what should stay specific to one childcare organization?
- How should the product be positioned commercially?

These are the questions of a training *system*, not a training *course*. Part Two shows how the platform answered several of them.

## 16. Where the ChatGPT record ends

The latest version in the ChatGPT record is best described as **a 12-week structured mentor development and mentoring system for early childhood education.** Its central mechanism is no longer the delivery of early childhood knowledge. It combines mentor development, 5 Voices and self-awareness, deliberate pairing, relationship building, communication, structured conversations, reflective practice, classroom observation and application, feedback and coaching, emotional intelligence, leadership development, collaboration, accountability, electronic reflection and evaluation, and ongoing coaching and support, aimed at teacher confidence, competence and retention.

In **September 2026**, the strongest website positioning in the ChatGPT work was:

> "Develop Teachers. Build Confidence. Inspire Retention."

# Part Two: From Design to Working System (platform record, 2026)

Part Two covers what was built. The ChatGPT record describes the *thinking* behind MSA. The platform shows those ideas turned into concrete content, tools and rules, and in several places pushed beyond them.

## 17. The Quality Interaction Framework: observation becomes a tool

**When:** before May 2026 (the tool predates the repository's history; its legacy tables are kept in the May 2026 migration).

MSA's first piece of software was not a course. It was an **observation tool**: the **QIF (Quality Interaction Framework)**. That fact says something on its own. Once MSA decided the mentor's job was to observe practice and help the teacher reflect on it (Section 8), the mentor needed a consistent way to see and record practice.

How the QIF works:

- **Observable indicators for each weekly topic.** Each focus area has a list of positive and negative teacher–child interactions to tally. For Warmth & Connection, positive items include "smiles at children," "greeting at the door," "gets down to child's level" and "shares a joke or story." Negative items include "yelling at children," "prolonged crying ignored," "ignoring child bids for attention" and "flat or disengaged tone."
- **A five-day weekly rhythm.** The mentor logs tallies on Days 1 to 4, noting the time of day. **Day 5** brings a coaching report, a goal chosen with the mentee, and a printable mentee resource for that week.
- **Goal suggestions tied to what was seen.** Each focus area offers concrete, observable goals (for example, "Greet each child by name at the door every morning this week" or "Replace one 'stop/don't' per hour with a positive redirection").
- **Research grounding.** Focus areas carry short research notes (for example, Vandell & Wolfe, 2000, on physical environments and language outcomes).

The first version identified mentors by a PIN, not a login, and stored mentees, tallies, weekly goals and PDF resources. It was a field tool for one mentor in one classroom, not yet a program platform.

**Why it matters for the paper:** the QIF made the "observe, don't just instruct" principle real. Feedback is now based on counted evidence ("12 minutes, zero warm exchanges") rather than general impressions. This answers part of the Section 15 question, "What data shows the program is working?", at the level of each classroom.

## 18. The content returns as a lens: the three-domain, 12-week structure

**When:** the structure is in place by May 2026. On May 18, 2026, topic labels were "aligned to MSA framework (distinct from CLASS terminology)." Week 4 was renamed "Emotional Safety & Repair" on September 10, 2026.

The platform organizes the 12 weeks into **three domains of four weeks each**:

| Weeks | Domain | Weekly focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | **Emotional Climate & Responsiveness** | Warmth & Connection · Teacher Responsiveness · Regard for Child Perspective · Emotional Safety & Repair |
| 5–8 | **Learning Environment & Organization** | Proactive Classroom Guidance · Maximizing Learning Time · Instructional Learning Formats · Learning Environment Design |
| 9–12 | **Instructional Depth & Language** | Concept Development · Quality of Feedback · Language Modeling · Higher-Order Questioning |

Three points matter for the paper:

1. **The ECE content came back, with a different job.** The April 2025 curriculum taught teachers ECE topics. In the platform, ECE quality is still the subject of every week, but it is **what the mentor observes and coaches on**, not what the mentor or mentee is taught. The question "What does a teacher need to know?" survives as the observation lens. The question "What does a mentor need to do?" became the training itself.
2. **The training is deliberately aligned to CLASS.** MSA's founders recognize the research showing that teacher–child interactions and the classroom environment are major drivers of classroom quality, and they aligned the mentor training to that research on purpose. The three domains follow CLASS's structure (emotional support, classroom organization, instructional support), and the training's focus areas use CLASS dimension names (for example, "Positive Climate · Warmth in Unstructured Time"). The alignment does two things. It **reminds the mentor**, every week, how much interactions and environment matter. And it gives the mentor a way to make sure **the mentee is gaining understanding, seeing the practice modeled, and developing their own skills.** In May 2026 the weekly topics were given MSA's own names. Week 4's move from "Negative Climate" to "Emotional Safety & Repair" (September 2026) also reframes the topic from avoiding harm to building safety and repairing it when it is broken. In October 2026 the reflections and the observation tool were updated to use the same weekly topic names as the training.
3. **The focus narrowed to interaction quality.** Several topics from 2025 (families and community, physical health, professional advocacy, identity) are not weekly topics in the platform. The 12 weeks now concentrate on the moment-to-moment interactions between teachers and children, which can be observed, counted and coached.

## 19. LEAP: from knowing your own voice to coaching someone else's

**Why "leadership expression."** The founders concluded that no single method, neither a natural "voice" nor a "leadership style," could reach their goals alone. A voice framework describes the emotional side of how a person shows up. A leadership-style framework describes the more intellectual side: how a person thinks, decides and works. MSA wanted to train to **both the emotional and the intellectual perspective**, so it combined them into a single construct: the **leadership expression**. This is the point where the 5 Voices work of 2025 (Section 7) was absorbed into something broader.

**When:** the full curriculum (12 modules × 16 scenarios) was in place and being refined through September 2026.

The mentor training is called **MSA LEAP Training**. LEAP stands for **Leadership Expression Assessment Profile.** In the platform, it holds the place the 5 Voices framework held in 2025.

**The profile.** Mentors and mentees each take the LEAP Quiz to find their natural leadership expression. There are four core expressions (**Driver, Inspirer, Sustainer, Cultivator**) and 12 blends (for example, Driver-Inspirer or Cultivator-Sustainer), for **16 profiles** in all. The center dashboard shows which mentors and mentees have completed LEAP.

**The training.** Each of the 12 weekly modules contains **16 scenarios, one for each profile**, for **192 coaching scenarios** in total. Every scenario follows the same structure:

1. **The mentee:** a teacher with a stated leadership expression and a short description of how they take in feedback.
2. **The scene:** a specific classroom moment (for example, a four-year-old offers a drawing at lunch and gets five words in return).
3. **Quality Check:** what is missing in the interaction, and why it matters for children.
4. **The coaching decision:** the mentor chooses among four coaching openers.
5. **Feedback:** the correct choice is explained as a match to *this mentee's* expression. Each wrong choice is identified as **another mentor type's natural move** (for example, "This is a Sustainer's natural move. It won't land with a Driver").
6. **Action steps:** concrete next moves for the coaching conversation and a trackable goal.

**Why this is a major step in the story.** The move to leadership expression did two things. First, it widened the lens from the emotional side alone to the emotional and intellectual sides together. Second, it changed the goal. In 2025, the 5 Voices work centered on *self*-knowledge: know your voice so you can lead yourself and others (Section 7). LEAP keeps self-knowledge, since mentors still learn their own expression, but makes it the starting point rather than the goal. The training goal is **adaptation**: recognizing your own default coaching instinct, seeing when it will miss with a particular teacher, and choosing what will actually reach that person. In the platform's words, the right approach "works regardless of your own natural leadership expression."

This is the "mentor as primary learner" decision (Section 12) worked out in full. What the mentor practices each week is not ECE content. It is the **coaching decision**: what to say first, to this person, about this moment.

**The Week 1 template, repeated.** The "perfect Week 1 first" decision (Section 13) shows in the result. All 192 scenarios share one fixed design, so the Week 1 design really did become the template for all 12 weeks.

## 20. Reflection with privacy built in

**When:** reflection content dated May 18, 2026. The reflection system is part of the "Platform v3" migration.

The platform makes the developmental reflection versus program evaluation distinction (Section 11) concrete.

**Three kinds of reflection:**

| Type | Who | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly** | Mentee | Four sections: *Previous Week Recap · Strategies Used & Outcomes · Goals for the Week Ahead · Data, Assessment & Progress* |
| **Daily** | Mentee and mentor | Short prompts on the day's focus. The mentee's daily reflection ends with a "bridge" note to carry into the weekly reflection. The mentor's asks about evidence of the mentee's growth *and* about the mentor's own coaching ("Was there a moment where you could have asked a better question or listened more deeply?") |
| **End of domain** | Mentee and mentor | After Weeks 4, 8 and 12: a look back across the domain, **plus** a 1-to-5 rating of the mentoring program and an invitation to give honest feedback |

**How the line between growth and evaluation is drawn:**

- Self-ratings in the daily and weekly reflections are about *practice* ("How would you rate the overall positive climate in your classroom this week? What evidence supports your rating?").
- Ratings of the *program* appear only at the end of each domain, three times in 12 weeks. That keeps product evaluation out of the everyday reflective conversation.
- **Who sees what.**
  - **Mentors** see their own reflections.
  - **Mentees** see their own reflections, and each mentee **chooses** whether to share any reflection with their mentor. They can change that choice at any time. The mentor home page shows only what the mentee has chosen to share.
  - **The two admins, Mary and Rebecca,** can read reflections, so the founders can follow how the program is working and what participants are experiencing.
  - **Program directors cannot read reflections.** The director dashboard shows only *counts* (for example, how many weekly reflections a mentor or mentee has completed), never the content.
- The mentee page frames it this way: *"These reflections are not evaluations — they are invitations."*

**Co-founder continuity.** Weeks 1 and 2 use Rebecca's exact wording from the **Inspired Growth Mentor Training Curriculum 12-Week Reflection Guide**. Weeks 3 to 12 use placeholder questions that follow her template while she finishes the guide. The April 2025 division of labor (Section 3) still holds in the platform: Rebecca owns the reflective and relational content, and Mary owns the observation content, the training scenarios and the infrastructure.

This answers part of the Section 15 question, "What should stay private between mentor and mentee?" The mentee decides. It also answers a question the 2026 redesign raised about the workplace: reflections are kept away from the people who supervise the participants. Directors can see *that* reflection is happening, which supports accountability, but not *what* anyone wrote, which protects relational safety. Only the program's two founders read the content.

## 21. From a mentor's tool to a program platform (May 2026)

**When:** the "MSA Platform v3" migration and the first platform commits, May 18, 2026.

The PIN-based observation tool became a multi-user platform:

- **Four roles with logins:** admin, program director, mentor and mentee. Each mentee is linked to a mentor, and each user to a center.
- **Centers with seats:** each center has a set number of mentor and mentee seats.
- **Existing QIF data kept:** the migration was written so it would not drop any existing observations.

Structurally, MSA was now built around **the organization**, not just the pair. That matches the workforce-system thinking of late 2025 (Section 10): the director, the center and the pipeline are now part of the software.

## 22. Ongoing support and the director's view (September 2026)

**When:** September 25 to 30, 2026.

- **Coaching Call Library.** Recorded coaching calls (for example, "Coaching Call #4 — Warmth & Connection") sit in a column beside the training modules. The 2025 idea of recorded sessions people can revisit (Section 9) is now part of the platform. New mentors can catch up and experienced mentors can return to them.
- **Director dashboard.** Directors see each mentor's training and mentoring progress and can step in early when someone falls behind. The director's role has grown from "protect the time" to "watch implementation."

## 23. Certification: mentoring becomes a credential (September 30, 2026)

**When:** September 30, 2026.

The 2025 offer included a **completion certificate** (Section 5). The platform replaced it with a **competency credential**, the **MSA Certified Mentor**:

- **Eligibility:** all 12 training modules completed.
- **The test:** 12 scenarios, one from each week, each with a different leadership-expression blend, drawn from the same scenarios mentors train on, avoiding ones the mentor has already been tested on.
- **Passing:** at least 75% (9 of 12). Each answer is locked in, and the mentor learns only whether it was right or wrong.
- **Integrity:** the training is locked while a test is in progress, and grading happens on the server, so answers cannot be looked up.
- **Annual calibration:** certification lasts one year. Recertification ("calibration") opens 30 days before it expires.
- **Retake rules:** three attempts back to back, then at most one a day for two more, then an admin must unlock it.
- **A formal certificate** with the MSA seal and the founders' signatures.

**Why it matters:** a completion certificate says a person *attended*. A calibrated annual credential says a person can *still make good coaching decisions*. Mentoring skill is treated as something that can be measured, can fade, and must be maintained. This answers part of "How standardized should the mentor protocol be?" The *judgment* is standardized and tested every year, while the relationship stays individual.

## 24. Public launch, pricing and the research trial (October 1, 2026)

**When:** October 1, 2026.

MSA launched publicly at mentorsuccessacademy.com with two ways in.

**The positioning moved again.** The September 2026 tagline in the ChatGPT record was "Develop Teachers. Build Confidence. Inspire Retention." The live site leads with:

> **Train one great mentor. Grow teacher after teacher.**

and puts this front and center: ***"The mentor is the one being trained."*** The site's three-step explanation (*The mentor learns · The mentor practices · The skills stay*) and its FAQ ("Who actually takes the training? The mentor.") say directly what took more than a year to work out: MSA's product is mentor capability that stays inside the program.

**The weekly commitment changed.**

| | 2025 model | 2026 platform |
|---|---|---|
| Mentor training | 30 minutes, live, weekly | Short self-paced module on the mentor's own time, plus the coaching call library |
| Mentor–mentee time | 30 minutes, paid | 30–45 minutes, protected by the director |
| Reflection | Reflection sheets | Mentee weekly, daily for both, end of each domain for both |

**Commercial model.** The 2025 model was a partnership between the founders (a 50/50 arrangement, Kajabi delivery, an internal pilot, then an external beta). By October 2026 it was a **center membership**: $249 a month for one mentor, with per-mentor discounts for 2 or more (down to $105 per mentor per month at 8 or more), annual certification included, and each mentor working with **one mentee at a time but unlimited mentees over the membership**. That last rule puts the pipeline idea from Section 6 into the pricing. A center pays for mentor capacity, not for each teacher served.

**The research trial.** The biggest change is in measurement. MSA now has a **free three-month research trial beginning January 2027**, part of Mary Wardlaw's doctoral research on mentoring in early childhood programs:

- Open to licensed, privately owned U.S. programs with at least two classrooms serving children 2½ and older.
- **Participating classrooms use MSA, and at least one classroom does not**, for comparison.
- Staff in **both** groups complete the same surveys **before and after** the program.
- Directors commit to 30–45 minutes a week for each pair for the full 12 weeks.
- Results are reported in summary form only, without naming programs or staff.
- Participating mentors receive training valued at $1,200 at no cost.

**Why it matters:** in Section 14, measurement meant 1-to-5 ratings and human review of reflections. In Section 15, "What data shows the program is working?" was open. The research trial gives a methodological answer: a **pre/post comparison-group design**. MSA has moved from *monitoring* its own implementation to *testing* whether it works.

## 25. Where MSA stands now (October 2026)

MSA is now a **mentor development system for early childhood programs**, made up of:

1. **A theory of change:** mentor capability, not content delivery, is what drives teacher growth, and it stays inside the program.
2. **A content lens:** three domains and 12 weekly topics in teacher–child interactions and environment, deliberately aligned to CLASS and named in MSA's own terms.
3. **An adaptive coaching curriculum:** built on leadership expression, which combines the emotional and intellectual sides of how people lead, with 192 scenarios teaching mentors to fit their coaching to 16 leadership-expression profiles.
4. **An observation instrument:** the QIF, with tallied indicators, a five-day rhythm, Day 5 coaching reports, goals and mentee resources.
5. **A reflection system:** daily, weekly and end-of-domain reflections. Each person sees their own, mentees choose what to share with their mentor, only the two founders read the content, and directors see completion counts only. Program evaluation is kept separate.
6. **Organizational infrastructure:** centers, roles, a director dashboard and a coaching call library.
7. **A credential:** the annually calibrated MSA Certified Mentor.
8. **A business model:** per-mentor center membership built around a growing number of mentees.
9. **An evidence plan:** a pre/post comparison-group research trial beginning January 2027.

# Part Three: Interpreting the Transformation

## 26. The transformation at a glance

| Stage | When | Central question | Main focus |
|---|---|---|---|

## 27. The central argument

MSA did not simply gain content over time. **Its underlying theory of how professional development happens changed.**

At first, the working assumption was roughly that **good content plus an experienced mentor produces a better teacher.**

Through implementation and design, that assumption gave way, one piece at a time:

- Knowledge transfer was not enough. **The developmental relationship mattered.** *(2025)*
- Mentors needed specific skills, beginning with **self-awareness and communication**. *(5 Voices, June 2025)*
- No single lens was enough. Mentors needed to understand both the **emotional and the intellectual** sides of how people lead. *(Leadership expression)*
- Learning had to reach the classroom, so mentors needed to **observe practice**, not just discuss ideas. *(Practice model, then the QIF)*
- Reflection needed structure, and it needed **protection** from evaluation. *(2026 redesign, then private-by-default reflections)*
- Self-awareness was a starting point, not the goal. The goal was **adapting to another person.** *(LEAP)*
- Mentoring skill could be **defined, tested and kept current.** *(Certification and calibration)*
- And finally, the system had to show **evidence of impact**, not just internal monitoring. *(Research trial)*

**The developmental arc:**

> Content → Mentoring → Relationship → Self-awareness → Leadership expression (emotional + intellectual) → Reflection → Observation → Application → Mentor competency → Adaptive coaching → Structured system → Credentialing → Evidence

**One more finding for the paper:** the ECE content was never discarded. It was **repositioned.** The topics MSA first set out to *teach* teachers became what mentors *watch for and coach on*. MSA's 2025 starting point and its 2026 result are about the same classrooms and the same children. What changed is who is being developed and how.

## 28. Points to verify before the paper relies on them

These are gaps or conflicts between the two records, or facts the platform shows but does not explain.

1. **When 5 Voices became leadership expression.** The reasoning is now recorded (Section 19). The date of the change, and any intermediate versions, would help the timeline.
2. **When the QIF was built.** The QIF predates the repository (first commit May 18, 2026). Its original build date and version history should come from Mary's own files or the ChatGPT and Claude conversations.
3. **Reflections for Weeks 3 to 12.** These are still placeholders awaiting Rebecca's final wording, as are the end-of-domain questions.
4. **The QIF's Week 4 focus.** The training and reflections call Week 4 "Emotional Safety & Repair." The observation tool still calls it "Conflict as Learning," and its indicators are about handling conflict between children ("stays calm during conflict," "guides problem-solving process"). This is a content difference, not just a label, so it was left as is. Decide whether the Week 4 observation indicators should be revised to match the training.
5. **Domain names differ slightly.** The reflections and the website use "Learning Environment & Organization" and "Instructional Depth & Language." The training modules and the observation tool use "Classroom Organization" and "Instructional Support." Pick one pair.
6. **Consent language for the research trial.** Trial participants should be told that the two founders can read reflections and that directors cannot.
7. **Mentee population.** The 2025 concept included new hires, struggling teachers and future leaders. The 2026 product is written for "mentors supporting teachers of preschool-age children (2½–5)." This narrowing (or focusing) should be named and explained.
8. **Topics dropped from the weekly sequence.** Families and community, health and physical wellbeing, and professional advocacy were 2025 weekly themes. Whether they moved to orientation, CDA or apprenticeship materials, or were dropped on purpose, belongs in the paper.
